Artisanal Gem Extraction in Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka's gem mining heritage is anchored in time-honored artisanal techniques that have been transmitted across generations. The sector remains largely dependent on small-scale, hands-on operations, demonstrating a commitment to both cultural continuity and ecological responsibility.

Open Pit Extraction (Illam Mining)

“Open pit extraction, referred to locally as “illam mining,” represents the most common and time-honored approach employed in Sri Lanka's interior gem fields, especially throughout south central areas. This technique requires vertical excavation through successive layers of surface material to access the illam, a productive gem-containing layer made up of decomposed gravels, clay, sand, and organic matter, typically situated several feet beneath ground level.”

Riverbed Mining

“Riverbed mining represents a traditional approach employed across alluvial plains and riverine environments, especially when seasonal water reduction occurs. This methodology capitalizes on the natural accumulation of gemstones that have been conveyed and settled by water movement throughout extended historical periods.”
